### Steel Authority of India Limited’s Evolution Over the Years

Remember, we talked about how SAIL is a big player in the steel industry and earned a special title called ‘Maharatna’ in the world of government-owned companies. This title is not easy to get. To be called a Maharatna, a company has to make a net profit of over ₹5,000 crore for three years in a row, have an average annual turnover of ₹25,000 crore for three years, or have an average net worth of ₹15,000 crore for three years.

Surprisingly, SAIL was established on January 24, 1973, and it wasn’t initially a fully operational company. But started the journey as a holding company, mainly responsible for overseeing government-owned steel plants in the country.

Currently, SAIL manufactures iron and steel at five integrated plants and three specialized steel plants. These facilities are primarily situated in the eastern and central regions of India, strategically placed near local sources of raw materials. SAIL is known for producing and offering a diverse range of steel products.

#### Steel Authority of India: A Story of Success and Growth

- 1954: Hindustan Steel Limited (HSL) was established, consisting of four plants: Bhilai Steel Plant, Rourkela Steel Plant, Durgapur Steel Plant, and Alloy Steel Plant.
- 1957: The Iron and Steel Ministry transferred the supervision of Bhilai and Durgapur Steel Plants to HSL.
- 1960: A Rail and structural mill was started at the Bhilai Steel Plant.
- 1962: By January 1962, HSL had a total steel production capacity of 2 million tonnes, and they had inaugurated their Wheel and axle plant at Durgapur.
- 1968: Construction of Bokaro Steel Limited began, and various facilities were commissioned at different HSL plants.
- 1972: To create a new industry management model, the Ministry of Steel and Mines shared a new plan with the parliament.
- 1972: The first blast furnace at Bokaro Steel Plant was inaugurated, Salem Steel Limited was formed in Tamil Nadu, and RDCIS was established in Ranchi.
- 1973: SAIL was officially incorporated on January 24, 1973, with an authorized capital of Rs 2000 crore, and started its operations with five integrated plants in Rourkela, Bhilai, Durgapur, Bokaro, and Burnpur. The Bokaro steel plant helped increase the overall crude steel production capacity to 4 million tonnes by 1973.
- 1978: Gradually, they started restructuring and transforming into an operating company by acquiring the Indian Iron & Steel Company (IISCO) as a subsidiary.
- 1985: The integrated trial run of the Meghahataburu iron ore project was inaugurated.
- 1986: The first modernization phase was initiated at Durgapur, and SAIL took over Maharashtra Electrosmelt Limited (MEL), subsequently renamed Chandrapur Ferro Alloy Plant (CFP).
- 1989: Then, in 1989, they formed The Raw Material Division, and the subsidiary Visvesvaraya Iron & Steel Plant (VISL) merged into SAIL.
- 1992: SAIL got listed in the Bombay Stock Exchange.
- 1995: Rourkela Steel Plant’s first modernization was commissioned.
- 1997: SAIL became a Navratna company.
- 2001: SAIL entered into a joint venture with NTPC to form NSPCL for captive power generation.
- 2004: SAIL released its environment policy, and the Bhilai plant produced the first 80-meter-long rail.
- 2006: IISCO was merged with SAIL.
- 2007: A modernization and expansion plan was initiated to increase crude steel production to 21.40 million tonnes per annum.
- 2010: SAIL became a Maharatna company.
- 2013: Durga, India’s second largest blast furnace with a capacity of 4060 cubic meters, was set up at RSP. Special grade steel from Bhilai and Rourkela was used for building India’s first indigenous aircraft carrier, INS Vikrant.
- 2015: Steel plants at Rourkela and IISCO were modernized and dedicated to the Nation.
- 2017: The Rail Mill at BSP produced the world’s longest single-piece rail, measuring 130 meters.
- 2018: LHB wheels were developed and supplied by DSP, as well as the new ‘NEX’ brand called Parallel Flange Section.
- 2019: In 2019, SAIL introduced its new range of branded TMT bars called SAIL SeQR.

In the fiscal year 2023, SAIL’s Integrated Steel Plants showcased diverse production profiles.
